\textbf{001. One manuscript, one problem family.}
The catalogue number is a local navigation label. The internal A1--E4
groups collect related assertions; they are not a count of separate papers
or independently new theorems.

\entry{The problem}{For a finite simple graph $G$, let ${}^{2}G$ replace each
edge by two parallel copies. Each copy receives red or blue. Whenever a
colour occurs on an edge, its two endpoints must have different degrees
in that colour. The original conjecture asks for two colours for every
connected $G\not\cong K_2$.}

\entry{Principal result: the subcubic case}{The manuscript presents a
computer-assisted proof of
\[
 G\text{ finite, connected, simple},\quad G\not\cong K_2,\quad
 \Delta(G)\le3\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\lir({}^{2}G)\le2.
\]
The proof combines structural reductions, prescribed local interfaces,
complete finite kernels and lifting arguments.}

\entry{Further sufficient conditions}{For simple $G$, the paper also gives
the following two sufficient conditions:
\[
 \delta(G)\ge8\chi(G)(2\chi(G)+1)-7,
 \qquad 8\delta(G)\ge7|V(G)|+6.
\]
Here $\delta$ is minimum degree and $\chi$ is chromatic number. Under the
second condition, the red degrees are distinct on all vertices and the
blue degrees are distinct on all vertices. The hypotheses and small-order
scope are exactly those in the full manuscript.}

\entry{Constructions and interfaces}{Further sections address sparse
branch cores, balanced subdivisions, clique root targets and block-tree
capacity, preserved colour counts, dense forbidden targets, complement
lifts, and signed-gap and parity-port constructions. Explicit examples
describe obstacles to specified stronger strategies. They are not
counterexamples to the original conjecture.}

\textbf{Scope.} The unrestricted conjecture for arbitrary connected
graphs is not proved. This is an AI-assisted research preprint, without
independent expert peer review or proof-assistant verification claimed.

{\LARGE\bfseries Materials and verification}\par
\entry{Read and build}{The complete 64-page manuscript, its sixteen TeX
source files, bibliography, build instructions and BibTeX citation are in
the \href{https://github.com/KevinMaSAI/doubled-graphs-local-irregularity/tree/main/preprints/Locally-Irregular-Two-Edge-Colourings-of-Doubled-Graphs-October-28-2026}{preprint directory}.
Run \texttt{tectonic main.tex} there, or run \texttt{pdflatex main.tex}
twice. The original PDF is unchanged by this catalogue update.}

\entry{Complete finite proof data}{The directly browsable A1 subset
contains five JSON certificates and six verifier modules. It covers
tree/cactus ports, sparse cores, two-marked and marked-theta interfaces,
fresh local checks and rejection of three deliberately damaged
certificates. The weak-two-marked kernel has 4,256 cases.}

\entry{The complete snapshot}{The frozen archive contains all 697
released files, including constructors, other complete certificates,
examples, provenance and previous reports. Additional checks cover 836
clique bases, four E1--E4 auditors recounting 972 saved colour copies,
and 1,114 bounded endpoint cases supporting the displayed general path
argument. These different checking layers have different scopes.}

\textbf{Standard-library commands, from the repository root:}
\begin{quote}\ttfamily\small
python -I -B proof\_artifacts/subcubic/verify.py\par
python -I -B verify\_snapshot.py
\end{quote}
Python 3.10 or later is required. These commands need no third-party
packages, network, solver or account. Do not use \texttt{-O}. Constructors
have separate dependency and scope instructions in the archive.

\entry{What the checks mean}{Complete finite calculations verify their
specified domains and witnesses. Bounded or random tests check only
their tested inputs. The infinite-family conclusions also depend on
the manuscript's structural and lifting arguments. Python certificate
checking is not Lean formalisation.}
